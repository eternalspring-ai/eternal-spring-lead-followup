# 30-day lead follow-up sequence drafts

## Ground rules (always)
- Draft only. Never send, schedule, or queue a message, and never call any tool that sends texts, emails, or DMs. Hand the drafts to the business owner to review.
- The business approves every message before it goes out.
- Plain, friendly language. No jargon.
- Never promise results (no "guaranteed", "double your sales", specific savings, or rankings).
- No data enrichment: do not look up, buy, or infer extra facts about a lead (income, credit, wealth, social profiles, etc.). Use only what the lead shared or the business provides.
- Never invent prices, availability, licenses, or reviews. Use [brackets] for anything the business must fill in.
- Every text and every email includes an opt-out: "Reply STOP to opt out" for texts, a clear unsubscribe line for emails.
- The sequence ends the moment the lead replies (any reply), books, or opts out. Say this at the top of the plan. After a reply, a person takes over.

## Ask first (if not given)
1. Business, service the lead asked about, and how they came in (form, call, ad).
2. Channels the lead agreed to (text, email, or both).
3. Next step the business wants (call, booking, quote).

## Default schedule (adjust if asked)
| Day | Channel | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Text | Thanks + one simple question |
| 1 | Email | Short intro and what to expect |
| 3 | Text | Gentle check-in |
| 7 | Email | Helpful tip or common question answered |
| 14 | Text | Offer a time to talk |
| 21 | Email | Simple recap and next step |
| 30 | Text | Polite last note, door left open |

No more than 7 touches. Don't send texts late at night; suggest roughly 9 AM to 8 PM in the lead's time zone.

## Output
A table or list: day, channel, subject (emails), message draft. Then a checklist for the owner: approve each message, confirm the lead gave permission, stop on reply.
